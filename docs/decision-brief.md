# Decision Brief: Engage v2 Root Cause — For Marcus

## Situation
30-day retention has dropped from 44% to 37% over the last two quarters, driven by a consistent pattern: users engage strongly in week 1, then go passive. This brief synthesizes three independent research streams — user interviews, NPS feedback, and a competitive scan — completed during discovery to diagnose the root cause before committing to a solution.

## Key Findings
- **The app feels static after week 1.** The home feed and insights don't change over time. Independently corroborated by user interviews (2 of 3 users) and NPS feedback (top complaint theme, 3 mentions).
- **Users don't know what to do with what they're shown.** They want to be told a next action, not just given more data — including an explicit ask for "a financial coach, not a spending tracker." Corroborated across interviews and NPS.
- **The app doesn't use what it already knows about the user.** One NPS respondent set a savings goal that was never referenced again — a concrete, fixable personalization gap, not just general staleness.
- **There may be real value on the other side of the passive period.** One 18-month power user reports that personalized pattern recognition and a self-formed weekly ritual took ~3 months to develop. Single-source and unvalidated, but suggests the current experience fails to bridge users to a real payoff before they churn.
- **No competitor owns this white space.** YNAB, Cleo, and Monarch Money each sit on one side of a tradeoff — effort-intensive but valuable (YNAB), or low-effort but only descriptive (Monarch), or proactive but not goal-aware (Cleo). None combine low-effort, prescriptive, and goal-aware.

## Options Considered
1. **Personalized, goal-aware weekly summary** — a low-effort, in-app touchpoint that surfaces one new insight, a specific next action, and progress toward the user's own goal. Directly targets all five findings above and the open competitive white space.
2. **Effort-based engagement model (YNAB-style)** — the highest-retention model in the category, but requires users to do more, not less. Cuts against direct user feedback and likely hurts activation for Nudge's current audience.
3. **Narrow fix: improve nudge/notification targeting only** — cheapest option, addresses a real complaint (notification opt-outs), but doesn't touch the larger static-experience and no-next-step findings that show up across all three research streams.

## Recommended Action
Move from diagnosis to a capacity-scoped pilot of the personalized, goal-aware weekly summary (Option 1), sized to Raj's available bandwidth, rather than committing to a full build or a broader effort-based redesign.

## Why Now
Retention is declining quarter over quarter, the root-cause signal has now converged across three independent sources — reducing the risk of building the wrong thing — and the competitive white space, while open today, isn't guaranteed to stay that way as rivals like Monarch actively ship new AI-driven insight features.
